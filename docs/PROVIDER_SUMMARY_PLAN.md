# Provider Summaries & Therapist Channel — Scoping Plan

**Status:** Draft for review (Session 70, 2026-09-25). No code written yet.
**Owner decisions so far (user, 2026-09-25):** Israel first · providers are **read-only** · the business goal is a **channel where therapists recommend DPNR** · a person may export a **PDF they own and are responsible for** · anything DPNR stores or displays to a provider must meet a **very high security bar** · **licensed professionals only** · **sharing is free** for the person · **a formal provider approval pipeline must be defined before any provider-side development** (gates Slice 2 onward).
**In progress:** Slice 1 (summary builder + PDF), started Session 70.

---

## 1. What we're building

Two things, delivered in order:

1. **Summary for my therapist (PDF).** The person builds a summary from their own DPNR material, reviews and edits every line, and downloads it as a PDF. It is generated in their browser; DPNR keeps no copy. Once downloaded, it is theirs.
2. **Share with my provider (on DPNR).** The person invites their therapist or psychiatrist. After the provider is verified and the person confirms them, the person can publish summaries to that provider, who reads them on DPNR through a hardened, read-only viewer. The provider sees only what was published: never live data, chats, or the Twin.

Around those two, the **channel**: verified providers get a free professional account, a referral link to recommend DPNR to clients, and a "Recommended by your therapist" onboarding path for those clients.

### Non-goals (for this plan)
- Providers writing anything back (notes, goals, care-plan edits). Read-only is the decision; a write-back feature would change Digital Twin logic and needs its own plan.
- DPNR acting as a clinical record, an EHR integration, or a monitoring service. No alerts to providers, ever.
- Diagnosis, risk scores, symptom scales or any clinical assessment generated by DPNR.
- Payments to providers per referral (see §9, ethics).

---

## 2. Principles (non-negotiable)

1. **The person authors the summary.** Nothing is included that they didn't see and approve. Every section can be removed; every line can be edited. Nothing is sent automatically, and nothing updates on its own after publishing.
2. **Client-prepared reflection, not a clinical document.** Every summary carries a header: *"Prepared by [name] using DPNR, a self-reflection tool. This is the client's own reflection, not a clinical assessment or diagnosis."* Wording stays inside the Intelligence Spec's non-diagnostic rules.
3. **A frozen copy, never live access.** Publishing creates a snapshot. The provider can't browse the person's account, and later sessions don't appear unless the person publishes again.
4. **Stronger encryption than the rest of the app.** Shared summaries are end-to-end encrypted from the person's browser to the provider's browser; DPNR's servers hold only ciphertext and can't read them (§5). That is a stronger guarantee than the rest of DPNR, which ADR 0016 describes honestly as encryption at rest with KMS-mediated access.
5. **Revocable and visible.** The person can revoke any share instantly, sees every time it was opened, and shares expire by default.
6. **Safety is not delegated.** Sharing never means someone is watching. The viewer tells providers plainly that DPNR doesn't monitor the client and that they should contact the client directly. Crisis flows stay exactly as they are.
7. **Minimum data.** Chats, unconfirmed signals, safety events and raw answers are excluded by default. Safety events can't be included at all in this plan.

## 3. The summary

### 3.1 Sections (the person picks a date range, then toggles sections)

| Section | Source (existing) | Default |
|---|---|---|
| A note to my provider ("what I'd like to talk about") | new free text, 2,000 chars | on, empty |
| Current focus / theme / direction | `RoadmapItem` (`content`: currentFocus, theme, direction) | on |
| Patterns I've recognized | `TwinSignalItem` with `status: confirmed` only, `description` | on |
| Room sessions in this period | `SessionSummaryItem.summary` for Decision + Mirror sessions in range | on |
| Commitments & goals | `CommitmentItem` (`description`, `status`, `reviewDate`, `lifeDomain`) | on |
| Life domains I've been exploring | Dashboard life-domain aggregation (confirmed signals) | off |
| Mood check-ins | **doesn't exist yet**: the Breath modal's mood chips are local-only | n/a until persisted (§8, later slice) |

**Excluded, not offered:** Main Chat transcripts, candidate/rejected signals, safety events/classifier states, credit history, raw step answers.

### 3.2 Composition
- **Slice 1 is assembly, not AI**: the builder lays out the person's own existing text in sections. No new model call, no new prompt, nothing to tone-review.
- An optional **AI-drafted overview** ("summarize this period in my words") is a later slice (§8). It would need a new prompt (`provider_summary/overview`), the reasoning layer's `core` tier, explicit non-diagnostic and no-speculation rules, and the person's edit before it's used.
- Language follows the person's locale (Hebrew RTL first-class). Session summaries are shown in whatever language they were generated in.

### 3.3 PDF (person-owned)
- Rendered **client-side** from the reviewed snapshot: a print-optimized route and the browser's own "Save as PDF" (no new dependency; Hebrew fonts and RTL already work in the app). No server copy, no upload, no log of contents.
- Before download: *"This file leaves DPNR. Anyone who has it can read it. Share it only with people you trust."*
- The PDF carries the §2.2 header and a generated-on date. No DPNR account data beyond what the person included.

## 4. Providers

### 4.1 Who is eligible (Israel, MVP)
Licensed professionals only, verified against official registries:
- **Psychologists:** Ministry of Health psychologists register (Psychologists Law).
- **Psychiatrists / physicians:** Ministry of Health medical practitioners register.
- **Clinical social workers:** Social Workers register.

**Decided (user, 2026-09-25): licensed professionals only.** Unlicensed psychotherapists, coaches and art therapists are out of scope.

### 4.2 Provider accounts
- A **separate Cognito user pool** (`dpnr-providers`), not a group in the client pool:
  - **MFA required** (TOTP; passkeys/WebAuthn next). The client pool's MFA stays optional.
  - Stricter password policy, short sessions: access token 15 min, refresh 8 h, viewer idle lock after 10 min.
  - Structural isolation: a client token can never call a provider API, or the reverse. No mixed roles on one account; a therapist who is also a DPNR client has two accounts.
- **Provider keypair**: an X25519 keypair generated in the provider's browser at signup, reusing `lib/crypto/keypair.ts`. The private key is wrapped the same way client accounts are (ADR 0014): under a DEK that is wrapped by a password-derived KEK (Argon2id) and a recovery code. **A stolen provider JWT alone can't decrypt anything**: opening a summary needs the private key, which only the provider's own credentials unlock in the browser.
- **Verification states:** `pending` → `verified` / `rejected`, plus `suspended`. Nothing can be shared with an unverified provider.
- **Formal approval pipeline: required, not yet defined, and blocking.** The user requires a formal approval pipeline to be defined **before any provider-side development** (Slices 2–4). It must specify at least: what evidence a provider submits; who reviews it and how the registry check is done and recorded; turnaround; rejection and appeal; periodic re-verification and suspension (license lapse, complaint); and the audit trail kept for each decision. The "internal admin script" once sketched here is superseded by whatever that pipeline defines.
- Profile: name, profession, license number, clinic/city (optional). Shown to the client before they confirm.

### 4.3 Provider experience
- **"For professionals" page** (Hebrew first): what DPNR is, what providers see (and don't), the security model in plain words, sign-up.
- **Provider dashboard:** clients who have shared with them, newest summaries first, and their referral link with aggregate counts only ("7 people joined with your link"), never names, unless a client chooses to share.
- **Viewer:** read-only, rendered from the decrypted snapshot in the browser.
  - Watermark with the provider's name, license number and the time on every page view.
  - No download or print button. Copy is discouraged, not prevented; §6 is honest that screenshots can't be stopped.
  - Banner: *"DPNR doesn't monitor your client. For anything urgent, contact them directly."*

## 5. Security design

### 5.1 Share encryption (end to end)
1. The person's browser builds the approved snapshot (JSON) from data it already reads through existing APIs. The server decrypts that data as it does today (ADR 0016); what's new is that **the snapshot itself is never sent in plaintext**.
2. It generates a random 256-bit **share key** `K` and encrypts the snapshot with AES-256-GCM (`lib/crypto/blob.ts`).
3. It wraps `K` twice, as sealed boxes (ephemeral X25519 ECDH → HKDF-SHA-256 → AES-GCM):
   - to the **provider's public key**, so the provider can read it;
   - to the **person's own public key**, so they can re-open exactly what they shared.
4. It uploads the ciphertext and the two wrapped keys. The server stores the ciphertext in S3 (SSE-KMS as defence in depth, a dedicated bucket, no public access, object lock off so revocation can delete) and the metadata in DynamoDB.
5. The provider's browser downloads the ciphertext and the provider-wrapped `K`, unwraps it with the private key, and decrypts locally.

**Revocation** deletes the ciphertext object and both wrapped keys. **Expiry** (default 30 days, person can choose 7/30/90/no expiry) does the same, via DynamoDB TTL and an S3 lifecycle rule, with a sweeper as backstop.

### 5.2 Threat model (honest version)

| Threat | Mitigation | Residual risk |
|---|---|---|
| DPNR database/S3 breach | Only ciphertext and wrapped keys are stored; no plaintext, no share keys | Metadata leaks: who shared with which provider and when. Treat the relationship itself as sensitive (§7). |
| Stolen provider session token | Decryption needs the private key, unlocked only by the provider's password/recovery code in the browser; MFA at sign-in; short tokens | A compromised provider *device* while unlocked |
| Malicious or compromised DPNR server substituting a provider's public key | Client shows provider identity (name, license) before confirming; public key fingerprint shown in "Security details" | A real limit: we serve the keys. Key transparency/pinning is later work, stated openly. |
| Provider leaks content (screenshot, forwarding) | Watermark, no download/print UI, per-view audit log visible to the client | Can't be prevented technically. Covered by provider terms and professional duty. |
| Share with the wrong person | Invitation is bound to a provider account that must be verified; the client confirms the verified identity before anything is shared | Client error at confirmation |
| Client coerced into sharing | Revoke any time, and every view is shown to the client | Out-of-band coercion |
| Enumeration / IDOR | Every share read checks ownership (client) or the provider-share grant (provider); share IDs are random UUIDs; the provider pool's authorizer is separate | Standard |

### 5.3 Controls
- A `security-review` pass is required before Slices 2 and 3 are called done (standing guardrail: auth + encryption).
- An **audit log** per share (`opened`, `revoked`, `expired`, with timestamp and provider ID), shown to the client in plain words. No content in logs, ever (standing guardrail).
- Rate limits on invite creation and share publishing, and on provider sign-up.
- Account deletion (client): `account/delete.ts` extends to delete all their shares, wrapped keys and invitations. Provider account deletion does the same for everything addressed to that provider.
- CSP stays strict. The viewer loads no third-party scripts at all.

## 6. Flows

### 6.1 Person → PDF (Slice 1)
Account → "Summary for my therapist" → date range → toggle sections → edit → preview → **Download PDF** (warning) → done. Nothing stored.

### 6.2 Person invites a provider (Slice 3)
1. "Share with my provider" → the person gets an **invite link** to send however they like (WhatsApp, email). No email service needed for the MVP; SES can come later.
2. The provider opens the link → signs up or signs in (MFA) → the account is `pending` until verified.
3. Once verified, the person sees: *"Dr. Maya Levi, licensed clinical psychologist (lic. 27-xxxxx), accepted your invitation. Share with her?"* → **Confirm**. Only now is the relationship active.
4. The person publishes a summary to that provider (the §3 builder with "Send to Dr. Levi" instead of Download). The person's browser encrypts and wraps it as in §5.1.
5. The provider sees it on their dashboard and opens it in the viewer. The person sees "Opened by Dr. Levi · Tue 14:02".
6. Revoke any share, or end the relationship (revokes everything addressed to that provider).

### 6.3 Channel: therapist recommends DPNR (Slice 4)
- A verified provider gets a referral link (`/r/<code>`). Signups through it get "Recommended by your therapist" onboarding copy, and the provider is pre-filled as a suggested invite. It's still inactive until the client confirms (§6.2 step 3).
- Attribution is stored as `referredByProviderId` on the client profile. The provider sees **counts only**.
- Possible incentives, all product decisions: extended free credits for referred clients, a free "Pro" provider account. **No money to providers per referral** (§9).

## 7. Data model (proposed)

Client partition (`USER#<id>`):
- `PROVIDER_LINK#<providerId>`: `status` (`invited` | `pending_verification` | `awaiting_client_confirm` | `active` | `ended`), timestamps. The relationship is sensitive metadata: stored plaintext because it drives authorization, but never shown to anyone except the two parties.
- `SHARE#<shareId>`: `providerId`, `createdAt`, `expiresAt` (TTL), `revokedAt`, `sections` (names only, no content), `s3Key`, `wrappedKeyForClient`.
- `SHARE#<shareId>#EVENT#<ts>`: audit events.

Provider partition (`PROVIDER#<id>`, in a new `dpnr-providers` table so the two data sets stay separate):
- `PROFILE`: name, profession, licenseNumber, verification status/metadata.
- `KEYS`: publicKey, wrappedPrivateKey, wrappedDek, wrappedDekRecovery (same shapes as the client `KEYS` item).
- `GRANT#<clientId>#<shareId>`: `wrappedKeyForProvider`, `s3Key`, `expiresAt`. This is what the provider's read authorization checks.
- `REFERRAL`: code and aggregate counts.

Invitations: `INVITE#<tokenHash>` (hash only, single-use, 14-day TTL).

S3: `dpnr-shares-<account>-<region>`, key `shares/<clientId>/<shareId>.bin`, SSE-KMS, lifecycle expiry, deny non-TLS.

## 9. Risks & open questions

**Needs the user / counsel:**
1. ~~Eligibility~~: **decided, licensed only.**
2. **Incentives for the channel**: which ones, if any? Payment per referral to licensed professionals likely conflicts with professional ethics codes (conflict of interest when recommending a product to a client); confirm with counsel before designing any incentive.
3. ~~Pricing~~: **decided, sharing is free for the person.**
4. **Default expiry**: 30 days recommended. Should "no expiry" be allowed at all?
5. **Provider approval pipeline** (blocking Slices 2–4): must be formally defined before development; see §4.2.
6. **Regulatory classification**: confirm with Israeli counsel how the Privacy Protection Law (as amended, Amendment 13) and the Data Security Regulations classify this data and what that requires (security level, access logging, breach notification, possible database-registration or DPO duties), and whether anything changes if EU residents use it (GDPR special-category data).

**Known risks:**
- **Scope creep toward a clinical product.** Anything that looks like assessment, scoring or monitoring moves DPNR toward medical-device and clinical-software territory. Principle 2.2 and the non-goals are the guardrail.
- **Server-served public keys** (§5.2): a real, disclosed limit of the MVP.
- **Metadata sensitivity**: that a person has a psychiatrist is itself sensitive. Keep link/grant data minimal and never expose it outside the two parties.
- **Hebrew-first provider UX**: all provider-facing copy needs native review. Clinicians will judge DPNR by it.

## 10. What "done" means per slice (acceptance)
- **S1:** a real account builds a summary in Hebrew and English, excludes every default-off section, downloads a correct RTL PDF, and no network request carries summary content to DPNR.
- **S2:** a provider can't sign in without MFA; a client token gets 401 on every provider API and the reverse; the private key is unrecoverable without the provider's password or recovery code; `security-review` is clean.
- **S3:** the server only ever receives ciphertext (verified by inspecting requests and S3 objects); an unverified or unconfirmed provider can't receive a share; revoke makes the next open fail and removes the S3 object; views appear in the client's audit log; account deletion removes every share; `security-review` is clean.
- **S4:** a referral signup is attributed; the provider sees counts only; the suggested invite stays inactive until the client confirms.
